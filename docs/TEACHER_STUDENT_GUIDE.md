# Vic Academy teacher and student guide

## Students

Register, verify your current email, and sign in. Project publishing and course enrollment require a verified Vic Academy account. Collaboration additionally requires acceptance of collaboration rules.

Open **My Courses**. Enter the course ID supplied by your teacher to request membership, or accept a teacher invitation by signing in to the course. Pending requests are visible to the teacher; only approved membership opens course materials.

Create your project and confirm publication rights and academic-integrity declarations. In the course assignment, read the rubric and deadline, then submit your project ID. A project requiring review stays private until teacher approval. Your feedback and grade are visible only to you and authorized teachers. If a submission is rejected, revise your work and resubmit before the deadline.

After collaboration approval, open the private project workspace. View tasks and milestones; contributor-level members can create, edit and complete tasks. Assign tasks only to approved eligible members. Losing verification, eligibility, or access removes workspace privileges immediately.

## Teachers

An administrator must assign the TEACHER role; users cannot grant themselves teaching access. In **My Courses**, create a course and share its course ID. Review pending memberships or invite an existing verified Vic Academy account. Invite another assigned teacher with the teacher course role to delegate course management.

Create class groups and add approved members. Create assignments with clear instructions, a deadline and a point-based rubric whose total matches the maximum grade. Enable review before publication when student work must remain private until approved.

Review submissions inside the course, record private feedback and grades, and approve/reject the submission. Optional public publication requires the creator’s rights confirmation. Export grades only when permitted by your school; the export contains private student feedback and must not be uploaded to public projects.

Create a private course collection or a public showcase. Public showcases accept only published public projects and hide any project later made private, archived or removed. They never include grades or private feedback.

Archive the course at the end of its lifecycle. Archiving hides course access rather than deleting projects or assessment records. An administrator can inspect records according to the school’s retention rules.
